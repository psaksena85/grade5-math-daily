The Secret Code of Numbers

Place Value to 10 Million Expanded Form Comparing Rounding

Imagine every number is written in a secret code, and the code has just one rule: a digit's power depends on where it stands. Move a soldier one spot to the left in the line-up, and it suddenly commands ten times as many troops. Learn to read that code, and huge numbers stop being scary — they're just a lineup of digits, each one shouting its own value.

This year we learn everything the same way, in the same order, every single time: Concrete (real objects in your hands) → Pictorial (a drawing that represents the same thing) → Abstract (the numbers and rules). This is how Singapore, one of the strongest math programs in the world, teaches every topic — so that's how we'll do it too.

STAGE 3 · ABSTRACT — THE RULES, IN NUMBERS

Now we take what your hands and the chart already showed you, and write it using math itself: expanded form, comparing, and rounding — three different jobs, same one rule underneath.

1 · EXPANDED FORM

Expanded form means breaking a number apart into what each digit is really worth, then adding those pieces back together. It turns a number from one confusing blob into a clear list of values you can actually reason about.

HOW TO DO IT, STEP BY STEP:

Step 1. Point at the first digit and say what place it's in (hundred-thousands, ten-thousands, thousands...).

Step 2. Multiply the digit by its place value (e.g. a 4 in the hundred-thousands place = 4 × 100,000).

Step 3. Repeat for every digit, then join them all with plus signs.

Same number from the chart above — now let's write out what it's really saying:

Worked Example
6,384,972

6,384,972 = 6,000,000 + 300,000 + 80,000 + 4,000 + 900 + 70 + 2

In powers of ten: (6×1,000,000) + (3×100,000) + (8×10,000) + (4×1,000) + (9×100) + (7×10) + (2×1)

Watch Out

A zero in a number still holds its place — it doesn't disappear. In 405,003 the ten-thousands and hundreds are both zero, but you still count that place when you're figuring out what the other digits are worth.

2 · COMPARING NUMBERS

Don't compare two numbers as a whole "blob" — your eyes will trick you. Instead, compare digit by digit, left to right, and stop at the very first spot where they're different. Whichever number has the bigger digit there wins, no matter what happens after.

Worked Example

Compare 5,462,908 and 5,462,890 — matching digits shown in white, the first difference highlighted:

vs
5,462,890 is bigger

Same first five digits: 5, 4, 6, 2, 9. Next digit: 0 vs 9. Since 0 is less than 9 — 5,462,890 is bigger, even though the numbers look almost like twins.

3 · ROUNDING

Rounding is place value plus one rule. Look at the single digit sitting just to the right of the place you're rounding to: 5 or more, round up. Below 5, round down. Everything after that turns into a zero.

Worked Example

Round 6,384,972 to the nearest hundred thousand

The hundred-thousands digit is 3. The digit right after it is 8 — 5 or more — round up. Watch what happens: the 3 becomes a 4, and everything after turns to zero.

Answer: 6,400,000

Chapter 1 Recap

  • Concrete → Pictorial → Abstract: how we'll learn every topic this year, not just this one.
  • Expanded form: break each digit into its place value, then add the pieces.
  • Comparing: go digit by digit, left to right — stop at the first difference.
  • Rounding: check one digit to the right of your target place. 5+ up, below 5 down.
